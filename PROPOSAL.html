Keelson Shorts Framework

How the shorts become one numbered series that walks the Keelson journey, lives inside the docs, and can be made one per sitting.

Today two shorts exist, FIG. 00 (what Keelson is, two minutes of motion graphics across eleven boards) and FIG. 01 (first workflow, 56 seconds of real footage). They were made by hand with the demo-shorts skill: five helper scripts plus a compose script rewritten for each short. The library sits outside the repo, the docs have no video on any page, and nothing records which short a reader should watch at which point in the journey. The first demo cut opens on a workflow, where the docs open on chat. FIG. 01 still has two quiet gaps and browser text that is unreadable on a phone; FIG. 00 sits low in the frame with labels too small to read.

This framework fixes the series before it grows. A series bible maps the figures onto the docs: sixteen in four acts (the plate, chat and projects, workflows, memory and usage, ribs) plus four standalone codas, so each short answers the question its docs page opens with. A short manifest replaces the per-short compose script, so a new short is a capture plus one YAML file. A publish step turns a finished master into a YouTube upload, a poster, a transcript, and an entry in a docs manifest that a <Short> component reads, putting the figure on its page and on a new Watch hub. The two existing shorts are re-cut through the rig first, which is how the rig gets proven.

Not in this change: the anti-sameness rule stays and the manifest fixes plumbing, never shape, so no two shorts share a structure by default. No 9:16 cuts until the 16:9 rail is done. The pipeline is not wrapped as a Keelson workflow until Act I is cut and the manifest has stopped changing. Production stops after every video for Daniel's review, and nothing posts anywhere without it. The FIG. 00 narration is not rewritten here; it is still waiting on his read.

The journey

The series is one sequence of figures in viewing order, grouped into acts that follow the shape of the product rather than the order of the docs sidebar: the plate, then chat and projects, then workflows, then what the harness keeps (memory and usage), then ribs. Chat comes first because it is the live half the docs introduce first, and a reader with nothing installed gets a real answer back before a YAML file is mentioned. Every figure maps to one or more docs pages, and the figure's opening line is the problem that page opens with. The mapping lives in each short's manifest, not in the figure number, so a tutorial stop that deserves two shorts gets two, and a concept or guide page with no stop (projects, governance, usage) gets its own.

Two forms carry the series, chosen per beat rather than per video. A plate beat is motion graphics drawn on the blueprint sheet, for an idea (FIG. 00 and the rib model are all plate). A demo beat is real footage of the product doing real work. A short may mix the two wherever a board explains a concept better than footage can, or footage proves what a board only claims; the form on each box below is the dominant one. Every short opens and closes with the series bookends and carries the FIG tag; the structure, opener, and devices vary per the ledger.

Viewing order is the number; the docs page is in the manifest. The existing first-workflow short becomes FIG. 04: it never speaks its number, so the change is one tag card, re-rendered in the re-cut it already needs. The tutorial rail keeps its own numbering.

The acts against the docs

Each act covers one cluster of docs pages, so the Watch hub's chapters and the docs sidebar name the same things. A reader who arrives from a concept page finds that act's shorts; a viewer who arrives from the playlist finds the pages the act was cut from. The one place the journey departs from the tutorial rail is memory: the rail teaches it at stop 4, between workflows and the multi-model build, and the series holds it until Act III so it sits beside usage as the two things the harness keeps. The rail is not reordered to match.

Every short makes clear what the concept is, why it exists, and how it works; it is not a recipe, and a viewer should leave understanding the thing rather than holding a list of steps. Each figure follows one spoken form so the series reads as one voice. Over the opener: "Figure N. Title." and the builder's problem in one sentence. Over the closer: the one-sentence answer, then the sign-off, "Keelson. Lay the keel, raise the ribs." A sheet card, "SHEET 04 · ACT II", is rendered per short like the fig tag and overlaid on the closer; it names the sheet and the act rather than a count, so a coda can be added without touching any other short. The shared closer clip and its close card are not changed. The bible that holds this, with the money shot and docs page per figure, is videos/SERIES.md; appendix A is its first version.

Where a short lives in the docs

A finished short is one master file: 16:9, 1080p, burned-in captions, loudness at -16 LUFS. The publish step takes that master and produces everything the docs and the channels need, so a page author writes one tag and nothing else. The docs never hold the video itself; they hold a poster, a transcript, and an id.

The mp4 never enters the repo. The docs manifest is written by the publish step, so the library stays the source of truth and the docs cannot drift from it.

The embed

The <Short> component is an Astro component that reads the manifest by figure number and renders the dark screenshot-figure frame the style guide already reserves for app footage. It shows the poster with the FIG tag and duration, plays on click through the privacy-enhanced YouTube endpoint so no YouTube script or cookie loads until the viewer asks, and writes the numbered figcaption from the manifest's concept line, which keeps the style guide's lead-in-plus-caption rule enforced by the component. Below the player a transcript disclosure carries the narration, so Starlight's search index and the generated llms.txt both learn what the short says. The page's own figure numbering stays as it is; the caption names the series number in its text.

The production line

Today the method is in the skill and the plumbing is rebuilt per short: FIG. 00 and the first-workflow short each carry their own compose script, card renderer, and timeline math. The change moves everything that does not vary into shared tools driven by one manifest, and leaves the creative choices where the ledger wants them.

The manifest describes a timeline, not a template: which scenes exist, where each line lands relative to a capture mark, which cards appear when. Structure, opener, and devices are still chosen per figure against the ledger.

The look of a demo short

One frame of FIG. 02 as the rig would compose it. The sheet, the header with the figure tag and progress counter, and the caption panel are the same chrome FIG. 00 already uses, so a demo and a plate read as the same series. The footage is cropped to the region the narration is about, not the whole window.

Three forms share this chrome: a plate draws on the sheet, a demo opens the viewport on the sheet, and a cold open can run full-bleed for two seconds before the sheet arrives. Full-bleed throughout stays a legitimate ledger choice for an X-first cut.

Bringing the two existing shorts up

Both are re-cut through the rig before any new figure is started. That is the rig's acceptance test: if the manifest and shared compose cannot reproduce two shorts that already exist, they are not ready for twelve more.

One file per short. Times are relative to capture marks so a re-capture does not move the narration. The composer asserts every line ends before the next starts and refuses to render otherwise.

C · Alternatives not chosen

AlternativeWhy not
Self-host the masters on Azure Blob or in the Pages siteNo captions, chapters, or discovery; repo and Pages bandwidth grow with every figure; a second place to keep in sync with YouTube anyway.
Git LFS for masters in the docsGitHub Pages does not serve LFS objects.
Number figures to match the tutorial rail one to oneStop 3 wants two shorts, and projects, governance, and usage have no stop at all; the manifest's docs field carries the mapping without constraining the numbering.
Open the demos on the first-workflow short as FIG. 01, since it is already cutThe docs introduce chat before workflows and a new reader has nothing to run a workflow against; the short never speaks its number, so moving it to FIG. 04 costs one tag card.
Remotion as the compose engineIt would unify plate and demo rendering in React and replace the ffmpeg filter graph. It also replaces a pipeline two shorts have already proven. Worth revisiting if captions, kinetic text, and overlays outgrow what the manifest composer can express.
Wrap the pipeline as a Keelson workflow nowLocks the first shape in before the manifest has settled. After Act I, when six more shorts have exercised it.
Inline autoplaying video on docs pagesBandwidth on every page view and a moving element beside the prose. Click-to-play on a poster, and the hub links to pages rather than playing.
Rely on YouTube captions and burn nothing inX viewers watch muted and X has no caption track for native uploads. Burned-in stays; the srt is extra.
9:16 from the startVertical is its own layout, not a crop. After the 16:9 rail exists, for the figures that earn it.
